Proclamation of Independence of Bangladesh

The Proclamation of Independence of Bangladesh was declared on the night of March 25-26, 1971, following the military crackdown by the Pakistani army known as Operation Searchlight. This historic document formally declared the independence of the People's Republic of Bangladesh (গণপ্রজাতন্ত্রী বাংলাদেশ).

The declaration was broadcast through various channels across the country, rallying the people of Bangladesh to resist the occupation and fight for their freedom. It marked the beginning of the nine-month Liberation War that culminated in the surrender of the Pakistani forces on December 16, 1971.

Key Excerpts from the Declaration:

"In view of the fact that the Government of Pakistan has waged an unjust war against the people of Bangladesh and in consideration of the fact that the people of Bangladesh are determined to establish a sovereign, independent state... we, the elected representatives of the people of Bangladesh, do hereby declare the independence of Bangladesh."

Historical Significance:

This proclamation served as the legal basis for the independence of Bangladesh and was later incorporated into the Constitution of Bangladesh. It established the fundamental principles of the new nation: equality, human dignity, and social justice.

The original document was preserved by the leaders of the provisional government at Mujibnagar and has since been recognized as a UNESCO Memory of the World document.

Source: National Archives of Bangladesh, Dhaka.
Condition: Original preserved; digital reproduction available.
Language: Bengali (original), English (official translation).
